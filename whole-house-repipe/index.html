Whole House Repipe in Parker, CO

A whole house repipe is a bigger decision than most plumbing calls, usually triggered by repeated pinhole leaks, persistently low pressure throughout the home, or a home inspection flagging galvanized supply lines. In Parker's older Mainstreet-area homes, original galvanized piping is well past its realistic service life. Parker Express Plumbing plans the repipe room by room to keep the home livable during the work and pressure-tests every run before closing walls.

The Parker Factor

Why This Happens Here

Homes near Parker's historic Mainstreet corridor predate the town's major 1990s-2010s growth and sometimes still carry original galvanized supply lines, which corrode from the inside and progressively restrict flow. Add Parker's hard water accelerating that internal buildup, and a repipe often solves recurring pressure and leak complaints that piecemeal repairs never fully fix. Homeowners in nearby Castle Rock deal with the same underlying conditions.

Signs To Watch For

Common Signs You Need This Service

Repeated Pinhole Leaks

Multiple unrelated leaks in different spots usually means the pipe material itself is failing.

Persistently Low Pressure Throughout The Home

Internal corrosion narrowing pipe diameter across the whole system, not just one fixture.

Discolored Water From Multiple Fixtures

Rust from galvanized pipe corroding internally.

Home Inspection Flagged Galvanized Piping

A common finding in Parker's older original-townsite homes.

Our Process

How We Diagnose And Fix It

We map the existing supply system, plan the new run to minimize wall and ceiling access needed, and install PEX or copper per the homeowner's preference and local code. Every new run is pressure-tested before we close up any access points. If the issue turns out to be related, we can also handle Slab Leak Repair during the same visit.

FAQs

Questions About Whole House Repipe

How long does a whole house repipe take?

Most single-family homes take 2 to 4 days depending on size and access.

Will I have water during the work?

We phase the work to keep at least partial water service running most days, with brief shutoffs during connection changes.

PEX or copper, which is better for Parker?

Both are code-compliant here, PEX resists the area's hard-water scale slightly better and costs less installed, copper has a longer track record.
