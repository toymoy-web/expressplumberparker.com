The Parker Factor

Why This Happens Here

Parker's 200 to 300-plus ppm hard water is tough on the rubber and plastic components inside fixtures, fill valves, and faucet cartridges. Mineral buildup causes seals to wear unevenly, which is why running toilets and dripping faucets show up more often here than in a soft-water market. Homeowners in nearby Lone Tree deal with the same underlying conditions.

Signs To Watch For

Common Signs You Need This Service

Running Or Phantom-Flushing Toilet

Usually a worn flapper or fill valve, often accelerated by mineral buildup.

Dripping Faucet

A worn cartridge or washer, small on its own but wasteful over months.

Low Water Pressure At One Fixture

Often a scale-clogged aerator or valve rather than a whole-house pressure problem.

Fixture Leaking At The Base

Usually a failed supply line connection or worn wax ring, not the fixture itself.

Questions About Plumbing Repair

Is a running toilet worth fixing right away?

Yes, a running toilet can waste hundreds of gallons a month, it is usually a quick, inexpensive fix.

Why does my faucet drip more here than it used to elsewhere?

Hard water accelerates wear on the rubber seals inside faucet cartridges, which is common across Parker.
